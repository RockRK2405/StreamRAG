# 03: Claim Extraction and Decomposition

**Code:** `generation/extraction.py` (`GeneratedClaimExtractor`), `claims/decomposer.py` (`ClaimDecomposer`, `ClaimLexicon`), `configs/claim_lexicon.yaml`

## Extraction from generated output

Structured output makes extraction deterministic: **one generated sentence = one candidate claim**. An output item that packs several sentences is split into one claim per sentence; the item's facts and labels apply to each part. (Verified as one unit, such an item failed entailment: the NLI model judges a three-sentence hypothesis or premise "neutral" even when each sentence is stated. Found in the first benchmark run.) The sentence splitter treats a "." before a digit as a decimal point ("4.5 mm").

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| text | inline markers the model wrote into the sentence ("… per shift. (E1)", "[E2, E3]") are removed. They are treated as cited labels, never as part of the statement; left in, they made the entailment model judge true sentences neutral (first benchmark run, arms C–E). |
| kind | `connective` for ≤ 4 words with no content term ("In addition:"); else `fact`. Uncertainty statements are never generated by the model (doc 07). |
| facts | the planned-fact ids it lists that exist in its section; unknown ids are recorded (`unknown_facts`) |
| cited evidence | evidence of its valid labels plus evidence of its valid facts; unknown labels are recorded as invalid (L0) and never become citations |
| importance | highest importance of its facts; without facts `important` if it carries a number / amount / date (*material*), else `supplementary` |

## ClaimDecomposer (atomic claims)

The decomposer is a rule-based, deterministic heuristic, driven by `configs/claim_lexicon.yaml` (grammar words only, no corpus vocabulary).

**Clauses.** It splits at ";" and at *and / but / yet* when the right side starts a new predicate:
- **elided subject:** "… and must be returned" → the left clause's subject is borrowed;
- **own subject:** "… and applications take 30 days";
- **coordinated participle:** "are submitted online and processed within 7 days" → "Applications are processed within 7 days".

"while" and "whereas" are not split: they usually subordinate a condition.

**Coordinated lists.** "P A, B and C S" → "P A S", "P B S", "P C S", when the items are short and contain no predicate. The first item's boundary (after the verb, or after a preposition) is chosen by parallel structure, matching the other items' length:
- "provide [proof of residence] and [a passport photo]";
- "logged with [the time], [the weather] and [any vessel]".

**Never split:** "or" lists (disjunction), and lists after a pair opener ("between the third and fifth week", "both A and B").

Examples (tested):

```
X requires A and B and applications take 30 days.   -> X requires A. | X requires B. | Applications take 30 days.
Applicants must submit A, B, C and D before applying. -> Applicants must submit A before applying. (x4)
Applications are submitted online and processed within 7 days.
                                                      -> Applications are submitted online. | Applications are processed within 7 days.
```

**Guards.** The heuristic can mis-split, so it never decides alone:
- **Evidence-side atoms** are used only if entailment confirms each one against its source (doc 01).
- **Generated statements** are decomposed only when the whole statement is *not* supported (doc 04). A bad split can therefore only remove content, never let an unsupported fact through.

**Known errors.**
- Subject borrowing can carry a relative clause ("Applicants who … and must provide X" → "Applicants who must provide X"). Such an atom simply fails verification.
- Clauses headed by a verb outside the lexicon are not split.
- English only.
